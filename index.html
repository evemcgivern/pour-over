    How the numbers work

    The finished-mug calculation estimates that coffee grounds retain about 2.1 g water per gram of coffee. For a 12 US fl oz mug at 1 : 16, that is about 25.5 g coffee and 408 g (13.8 US fl oz) brew water. Retention varies by coffee, grind, and brewer, so treat this as a starting estimate. Source: coffee brewing control chart study.

    How taste can guide adjustments

    Ratio changes cup strength and can also affect extraction. Grind and pouring affect extraction too.

    • Weak or watery: use a little less water per gram of coffee. For example, move from 1 : 16 to 1 : 15.
    • Sour or sharply bright: try a slightly finer grind to extract more.
    • Bitter or drying: try a slightly coarser grind to extract less.

    Change one thing at a time. The study found sour, fruitier notes were strongest at high strength with lower extraction; bitterness and astringency at high strength with higher extraction. These are tendencies, not guaranteed outcomes.

    Based on UC Davis drip-coffee sensory research; your coffee and brewer may differ.

    Pour-over ratio basics

    What is a good starting coffee-to-water ratio for pour-over?

    1:16, meaning 1 g of coffee to 16 g of water, is a common starting point and is the default in Live Brew. The calculator lets you choose any ratio from 1:14 to 1:18.

    How much coffee do I need for 500 g of water at 1:16?

    About 31 g of coffee. Divide the water by 16: 500 g / 16 = 31.25 g.

    How do I make my pour-over stronger or weaker?

    Change the ratio. Use a little less water per gram of coffee for a stronger cup, for example moving from 1:16 to 1:15. Use a little more water for a weaker cup.

    How much water do coffee grounds keep?

    Grounds retain about 2.1 g of water per gram of coffee, so a finished mug holds less than the brew water you pour. Live Brew uses this estimate in finished-mug mode; retention varies by coffee, grind, and brewer. Source: UC Davis coffee-brewing control chart study, https://doi.org/10.1111/1750-3841.16531.

    My pour-over tastes sour or bitter. What should I change?

    If it tastes sour or sharply bright, try a slightly finer grind to extract more. If it tastes bitter or drying, try a slightly coarser grind to extract less. Change one thing at a time.
